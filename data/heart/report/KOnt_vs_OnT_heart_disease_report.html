K-Ont research update · heart-disease verification

Does K-Ont help machine learning more than OnT?

A controlled comparison of standard OnT and K-Ont (OnT extended to knowledge-graph individuals) as patient representations for heart-disease prediction, with four classic machine-learning models and a neural network.

1. The question

Our battery-materials work showed that K-Ont, our extension of the Ontology Transformer (OnT) to knowledge-graph individuals, represents a knowledge graph better than standard OnT, which only embeds ontology classes. The guide asked whether this carries over to a practical task: if a machine-learning model is given ontology embeddings, does K-Ont improve its predictions more than OnT does? This report tests that on a second, independent domain: heart-disease diagnosis.

2. Data

  • Patients: 303 patients from the UCI Cleveland heart-disease data, 13 clinical features (age, sex, chest pain type, resting blood pressure, cholesterol, fasting blood sugar, resting ECG, maximum heart rate, exercise-induced angina, ST depression, ST slope, number of major vessels, thallium scan) and a binary label (heart disease or not; 139 / 164).
  • Ontology: 32 classes (e.g. typical angina ⊑ chest pain ⊑ abstract ⊑ entity) and 5 relations (has gender, has chest pain, has resting ECG result, has ST slope, has thallium scan result).
  • Knowledge graph: the ontology plus the 303 patients as individuals, each with its 13 facts and a hasDiagnosis link. The graph lists patients in the same order as the table; ages were checked one by one. One ECG value, "ST wave anormality" (4 patients), has no class in the ontology and was mapped to its class "wave abnormality".

3. Method

Representations compared

  • Raw table (baseline): the 13 features, categorical ones one-hot encoded, numeric ones standardised.
  • Standard OnT: OnT trained on the ontology alone, with OnT's own DeepOnto data preparation (30 subsumption axioms), 20 epochs. OnT has no representation for an individual, so a patient is the concatenation of the OnT vectors of its five categorical values.
  • K-Ont: OnT trained on the ontology plus the training patients as knowledge-graph individuals. Each patient is one sentence of its 13 facts (shown in section 4), trained as an instance of "patient" and linked by the five relations to its value concepts, with in-batch negatives; ontology rows are 15% of training; 3 epochs. A patient's representation is K-Ont's encoding of its sentence.
  • Plain encoder (control): the untrained starting model (OnT-MiniLM-galen) reading the same patient sentence. It separates the value of K-Ont's training from the value of simply encoding the patient as text.

Leakage control

The diagnosis relation is removed from the ontology and never written into any sentence or training row. K-Ont is retrained inside every cross-validation fold on that fold's training patients only, so test patients are only ever encoded, never trained on. All vectors are mapped to the tangent space (logmap at the origin) and reduced to 16 numbers by PCA fitted on the training fold.

Classifiers and evaluation

Logistic regression, random forest (300–500 trees), RBF support-vector machine, histogram gradient boosting, and a multilayer perceptron (2 × 64 units, dropout 0.2, early stopping on an inner validation split, averaged over 3 seeds). Stratified 5-fold cross-validation repeated twice gives 10 folds, identical for every model and feature set. Metrics: ROC-AUC (main), accuracy and F1. Differences are paired fold by fold and tested with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test; "significant" means p < 0.05.

5. What the embeddings contain

Where each model places patients in the ontology

For every patient, the ontology class nearest to it by OnT's own hierarchy score. The correct answer is "patient". K-Ont places all patients, including the 61 test patients of fold 0 it never trained on, under "patient"; standard OnT, which was never shown an individual, places them near "disease status".

Hierarchy in hyperbolic space

Distance from the centre of the Poincaré ball for every class, grouped by its level in the ontology (level 0 = top classes such as "entity"), and the median patient. In a well-formed hyperbolic embedding general classes sit near the centre and specific ones further out. K-Ont largely keeps this ordering and places patients, the most specific things, furthest out; OnT's classes are spread with little order.

Reading. The hypothesis that pretrained embeddings help when labels are scarce does not hold here: with 10–50% of the labels, adding K-Ont lowers AUC, significantly in most models. The embeddings carry no information beyond the table, so the extra 16 numbers per patient only give the classifier more to overfit on when it sees 24 to 120 labelled patients.

8. Test 3: columns missing from the table

Chest pain type and thallium scan (and, in the second case, the number of major vessels) are removed from the table the classifiers see, while the knowledge-graph embeddings still contain them. OnT's concept vectors hold chest pain and thallium but cannot hold the number of vessels; K-Ont's patient sentence holds all three.

9. Conclusions

  • K-Ont outperforms standard OnT as a representation of knowledge-graph individuals. It types unseen patients correctly, keeps patients distinct, beats chance on all five relations, and its vectors predict the outcome better than OnT's. This agrees with our battery-materials results.
  • A knowledge-graph embedding helps a classifier when the graph holds information the classifier's input lacks (test 3). It does not help when the input already contains everything (test 1), and it hurts with very few labels when it adds no new information (test 2).
  • K-Ont's training adds little beyond encoding the patient as text on this dataset. The heart ontology is shallow and states no medical knowledge, so there is little for training to inject. The added value of K-Ont over OnT here is that it can embed individuals at all.

Limitations

  • One small dataset (303 patients) and a shallow ontology; 10 folds give limited statistical power for differences of 1–2 AUC points.
  • One K-Ont configuration (3 epochs, 16 PCA components); no tuning was done for either OnT or K-Ont, to keep the comparison fair.
  • The relation check uses one fold's model; typing and maps use fold 0's K-Ont.

Recommended next steps

  • Test on a knowledge graph with real domain knowledge that the table lacks: risk factors, drug–condition links, comorbidities, or links between patients.
  • Report the missing-information setting (test 3) as the main use case of knowledge-graph embeddings, and keep the plain-encoder control in every comparison.
  • For the battery project, the same lesson applies: K-Ont adds most where the knowledge graph carries structure or knowledge that the generator's prompt does not already contain.
